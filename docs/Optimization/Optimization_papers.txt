2) Optimization of 3×3 inch NaI(Tl) detector related to energy, distance and
bias voltage 

The author searches the correlation between the energy of the incident photons, the distance between the source and the detector and lastly the HV to the PMT.

    a) Efficinecy @ Gamma energy: peak efficiency has a non linear behaviour, at 
       low energy peak efficieny is higher, it reaches a maximum and than goes 
       down
    b) Source-Detector distance: the more the distance, the efficinecy decreases 
       because the solid angle decreases
    c) HV: optimal if the collected electric charge is linearly proportional to 
       the gamma energy. Not optimal HV values can cause degradation in the enegy 
       resolution
       
3) Optimizing Pulse Height Resolution Measurements 

(I write only the feasible things)

Problems for PMT:
    a) There is no magnetic or electrostatic shielding. In this case,
       re-orienting the PMT with respect to the earth’s magnetic
       field will result in a pulse height shift. Installing the PMT
       in a mu-metal shield and attaching the shield to system
       ground is a common solution.
    b) The PMT is operated at too high of a voltage. This results
       in anode saturation. This may be diagnosed by viewing
       the anode output directly on the input of an oscilloscope
       terminated in 50Ω. View the pulse amplitude at the
       energy to be measured. Then place a source with gamma
       ray energy roughly twice the previous source and view its
       pulse amplitude. The ratio of the viewed pulses should
       equal the ratio of the gamma ray energies. If not, then
       the anode is probably saturating
       
Problems for Preamp:
    a) The Preamp is saturating. This can be viewed by looking at the
       preamp output at the high impedance input of an oscilloscope. 
       Increase the high voltage with a source present and find that voltage 
       above which the preamp pulse clips. To prevent saturation, the PMT 
       should be run at substantially lower voltage than this clipping voltage.
    b) The cable leading from the PMT signal output to the preamp input is too 
       long. A longer cable increases the capacitance at the input of the 
       preamp. This increased capacitance may have an effect on the noise
       characteristics of the preamp. Between 1 and 3 feet, or 
       as short as practical, is the recommended length for this cable
       
Problems for Amp:
    a) The wrong integrating time constant is used. Scintillators with 
       longer decay times require longer integrating times. As a rule of    
       thumb, the integrating time constant is set at 4 x the 1 /e decay time 
       of the scintillator.

Problems with determining resolution:
    a) MCA is not zeroed
    b) MCA offset
    c) The wrong pulse shape is used at the input of the MCA. Some MCAs are 
       designed to work with a unipolar input pulse and others are designed 
       for a bipolar input
