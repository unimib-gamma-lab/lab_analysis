*Critical parameters*

1) Dead_time = 8e-6; meaning: time for the hardware to elaborate each single impulse and register in the memory. It can be useful e.g. to verify dead time

2) Dead time correction: selectable between "Extended Live-Time (Gedcke-Hale) and "Simple Live-Time". The first one seems to be more precise. If I can, I study it a little more to understand its principle.

3) Conversion gain (number of channels): depending on the model, for the 2k version we can choose via software 256, 512, 1024, 2048 channels, while for the 8k version we can arrive till 4096 and 8192.
Suggestion: considering NaI has generally low energy resolution, choosing to high channels risk to "saturate" (bins to thick with low information and much Poisson noise)

4) Electronic gain instability = <= 50 ppm/°C (can be used to compute the systematics for temperature)

5) Inputs (All 1 kOhm resistance): 

Input: Accepts unipolar positive impulses between 0 and 10 V (maximum at 12V ?). Time rising between 0.25 and 30 microseconds (not a problem for a us).
This means that the 1274 peak must be, in Volt, below 10 V, this sets a maximum for the gain or HV supply.

ADC gate accepts slow positive NIM imput. On the computer we can select Coincidence and Anticoincidence
Signal must occur prior to and extend 0.5 μs beyond the peak of the pulse;

PUR: Pile up rejection input, accepts slow-positive NIM signal; seems cool, we have to see if there's a way to use it

BUSY: busy input used by live-time correction circuits, accepts slow-positive NIM signal, signal must occur prior to peak detect



6) Software controls (actually regards Maestro): ADC LLD (Low Level discriminator) and ADC ULD (Upper Level Discriminator) to cut for example the electrical noise.

7) "Successive-approximation con sliding scale linearization": low differential non-linearity (<= +-1%), this means the calibration should be approximately linear
Integral non-linearity is <=+-0.025% over the top 99% of the dynamic range

8) Memory of 2^31 - 1 counts per channel, i.e. ~ 2 billio
